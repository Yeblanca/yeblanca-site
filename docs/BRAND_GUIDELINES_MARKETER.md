# Yeblanca Brand & Copy Guidelines

*For the marketing team — no code, no jargon. Just the visual and writing rules you need to create strategy and content.*

---

## The Vibe in One Sentence

> **Y-3 meets Yeezus meets Saint Laurent — applied to web development.**

Cold, geometric, minimal, expensive-looking. A quiet dark room with expensive furniture and a single pink flower. Nothing bounces, nothing floats. Just precision.

---

## Color Palette

### Backgrounds & Surfaces (Dark Mode — this is our default identity)

| What | Color | Looks Like |
|---|---|---|
| Main background | Almost black `#0a0a0a` | Soft black, not pure #000 |
| Cards, boxes, inputs | Dark gray `#111111` | One step lighter than background |
| Hover states | Darker gray `#1a1a1a` | Slight lift on interaction |
| Text | Off-white `#f0f0f0` | Not pure white — softer on the eyes |
| Secondary text | Muted white at 65% opacity | Subtle, receded |
| Lines & borders | White at 8% opacity | Barely there, very delicate |

### Light Mode (exists as an alternative, but dark is who we are)

| What | Color |
|---|---|
| Background | Light gray `#f5f5f5` |
| Cards | White `#ffffff` |
| Text | Almost black `#0a0a0a` |

### The Pink — Our Signature Color

**`#FF3E7F`** — Hot pink. This is our only accent color. It never changes between dark and light mode.

| When to use pink | Examples |
|---|---|
| Primary buttons | "Start a Project", "Get a Quote" |
| Active navigation link | The page you're currently on |
| Featured cards | A special/featured item gets a pink left border |
| Section dividers | The little line before section titles |
| Hover effects | Links turn pink when you hover |
| Focus outlines | Tab-targeting an element shows a pink ring |

**Golden rule:** Beyond pink, everything is black, white, or gray. No blue for info. No green for success. No red for errors (we use pink for errors too). Just pink + grayscale.

---

## Typography

### Primary Font: Space Grotesk

This is our "voice" font. Clean, geometric, modern.

| When | Style |
|---|---|
| Big headlines | Bold (700), all caps, tight letter spacing |
| Section titles | Medium weight (500), slightly loose |
| Body text | Light weight (300), generous line spacing, slightly faded |

### Secondary Font: Space Mono

This is our "technical" font. Used for labels, badges, indexes, small metadata.

- Always in ALL CAPS
- Always with extra letter spacing (spread out)
- Always slightly faded (45-55% opacity)
- Think: industrial, engineering, precision

### When to Use Which

| Content | Font |
|---|---|
| Headlines, titles, body paragraphs | Space Grotesk |
| Navigation links | Space Mono |
| Price tags, labels, badges | Space Mono |
| "01", "02" indexes | Space Mono |
| Small print, meta info | Space Mono |
| The YEBLANCA logo | Space Grotesk Bold |

### Font Sizes (Approximate)

- **Body text:** 16px
- **Small labels:** 12px
- **Section titles:** 20-24px
- **Page titles:** 30-36px
- **Hero / big display:** up to 48px (smaller on mobile)

---

## The 2px Rule (Very Important)

**Everything — and we mean everything — uses a 2px border radius.**

Buttons, cards, input fields, badges, all of it: just a tiny hint of rounding (2px). Not fully rounded, not perfectly square. This 2px corner is our signature silhouette.

The only exception: **profile avatars** can be fully circular.

---

## Buttons

| Type | Look |
|---|---|
| **Primary** | Solid hot pink background, white text, ALL CAPS, widely spaced letters |
| **Ghost** | Transparent background, thin white border, white text |
| **Danger** | Transparent background, thin pink border, pink text |

No button has a shadow. No button has a gradient. Just flat color and a 2px corner.

---

## Cards & Boxes

| Type | Look |
|---|---|
| Default card | Dark gray background (`#111111`), ultra-thin border (half a pixel), 2px corners |
| Featured card | Same as default PLUS a 2px hot pink line on the LEFT side |

No card has a shadow. No card lifts or floats on hover. On hover, the border color quietly shifts.

---

## What We Do NOT Do (Hard Rules)

| ❌ Never | Why |
|---|---|
| Gradients | Flat colors only. No fades, no ombre. |
| Shadows / drop shadows | We use thin borders instead of shadows for depth |
| Fully rounded corners (except avatars) | Our signature is the 2px corner |
| Blue for links/info | Pink is our only accent. Blue is not part of our palette. |
| Green for success | See above. Use opacity changes instead. |
| Cards that float or pop up on hover | No 3D effects, no lifting. Just subtle border color changes. |
| Big background images in the hero section | Flat dark/light background only. No photos as backgrounds. |
| Fonts bigger than 48px on mobile | Headlines must scale down on small screens. |

---

## Layout Philosophy

**Luxury brands don't fill the viewport.**

We leave generous empty space around everything. Our content maxes out at a reasonable width (64rem / about 1024px). The empty space isn't empty — it's framing. It makes the content feel expensive.

Section spacing:
- Tight screens (mobile): lots of vertical padding
- Wide screens (desktop): even more vertical padding

---

## The Overall Feel

Imagine walking into a gallery:

- Dark walls
- One spotlight on a single piece
- A single pink accent — a flower, a line, a button
- The furniture (cards, buttons, text) has sharp, precise edges
- Nothing moves unless you interact with it
- When you do interact, the response is subtle: a color shifts, an opacity changes
- No bouncing, no wobbling, no fireworks

**This is not for everyone. It's for people who understand that restraint is a form of luxury.**

# Copywriting & Writing Direction

*How yeblanca sounds — for strategy, posts, captions, and every word we publish.*

---

## The Voice in One Sentence

> **Confident, direct, and minimal — like an engineer who's seen too many bad systems and isn't afraid to say so.**

We don't sound like a marketing agency. We sound like someone who builds things that work and has strong opinions about how software should be made. Not arrogant. Just sure of what we know.

---

## Brand Personality Spectrum

| Axis | Where we land |
|---|---|
| Formal / Casual | **Casual but not sloppy.** We say "you" and "we." No corporate "one." |
| Warm / Cold | **Cool but not unfriendly.** We're direct, not rude. |
| Technical / Simple | **Simple, but we don't dumb it down.** We explain things plainly. |
| Hype / Restraint | **Restrained.** We don't scream. We don't use exclamation marks unless it's earned. |
| Jargon / Plain | **Plain.** "System" not "solution." "Build" not "engineering lifecycle." |
| Humble / Confident | **Confident.** We know what we're good at. We're also clear about who we're not for. |

---

## The Tone Rules

### 1. Short sentences. Short paragraphs.

Look at the website copy. Almost every sentence is on its own line. We write like we design: with space. A block of text should feel breathable.

> Good: "You've outgrown the off-the-shelf solution."
>
> Not: "When your current platform no longer meets your evolving business requirements..."

### 2. Say what you mean. No padding.

We don't say "leverage," "optimize," "synergize," "holistic," "best-in-class," or "seamless." We say "build," "run," "fix," "ship."

Bad words (never use):
- "Solution" (unless it's a literal chemical solution)
- "Leverage"
- "Empower"
- "Ecosystem"
- "Transformative"
- "Journey"
- "Robust"
- "Scalable" (show it, don't say it)
- "World-class"
- "State-of-the-art"

### 3. Talk directly to the person reading.

Use "you." Use "your business." Write like you're in the same room.

> "You've never had a website and you're not sure where to start."
>
> "You're running your operation on spreadsheets, WhatsApp, and willpower."

### 4. Be honest about what you're not.

We filter people out. That's the point. Headlines like "yeblanca is not for everyone" are not weaknesses — they're positioning.

> "If you need a website in 48 hours, we're not it."
>
> "If your budget is $300 and your expectations are enterprise, we're not it."

This honesty makes the people who ARE a fit trust us more.

### 5. Use the pink sparingly in words too.

The visual pink is the single accent. The verbal equivalent is: one bold claim, one sharp sentence, one provocative statement. Not everything can be the accent. Most of your writing should be muted — like the foreground text at 65% opacity.

---

## The Bilingual Rule

We write in **English and Spanish**. Not translated — written.

- English is our default business language
- Spanish is not a translation. Each version is written for its audience.
- Some content can be bilingual (mixing both) — especially on social media, where code-switching reflects who we actually are
- The border (Texas-Mexico corridor) is our context. Writing that lives in both languages is authentic to the brand.

**On social:** A post in Spanish is fine. A post in English is fine. A post that opens in one and lands in the other is even better. Example:

> "Tu negocio merece un sistema que funcione, no otro parche.
>
> We build custom systems for businesses operating between Mexico and the U.S. No agencies. No platform fees. No BS.
>
> Hablemos."

## Content Pillars (What We Talk About)

These are the themes you'll build posts around:

### Pillar 1: The Border Position
> **We build for the Texas-Mexico corridor. Not as a niche — as our context.**

Post ideas:
- The reality of running a business in Piedras Negras / Eagle Pass / Laredo
- Cross-border logistics, payments (OXXO + Stripe), bilingual customers
- Why "bilingual" is the minimum, not a differentiator, here
- Businesses that serve both sides of the border

### Pillar 2: The Anti-Agency
> **No account managers. No middlemen. The engineer who scopes it builds it.**

Post ideas:
- What happens when there's no "account manager" layer
- The problems with agency models (translation loss, junior devs on week 3, status meetings instead of progress)
- Why "micro-consultancy" is different from "agency"
- Direct comparisons: yeblanca vs. typical agency vs. DIY platforms

### Pillar 3: Build It Right
> **Clean code, strict TypeScript, zero shortcuts. Built to last, not built to be replaced in 18 months.**

Post ideas:
- What "built to last" actually means in software
- Why we don't use WordPress, Wix, or drag-and-drop builders
- The cost of rebuilding vs. building right the first time
- Open source philosophy, no vendor lock-in
- Why your Shopify store charging 2% + transaction fees is eating your margin

### Pillar 4: The Three Deliveries
> **Hosted / Managed / Architect — not every business needs to own the engine to drive the car.**

Post ideas:
- Explain each tier with real examples
- Who should choose which
- The Architect tier: "you own every line, deploy it anywhere, with anyone, forever"
- Why some businesses SHOULD own the code and others SHOULDN'T

### Pillar 5: Real Problems, Real Fixes
> **You're running your operation on spreadsheets, WhatsApp, and willpower.**

Post ideas:
- Specific problems: appointment scheduling via notebook, lost leads, manual invoicing
- Before/after (not visually, but operationally)
- The cost of manual chaos (3-5 hours/week lost)
- Industry-specific: trucking companies, medical tourism, local retail, salons

### Pillar 6: Founder / Origin
> **Built at the border. For businesses that live there.**

Post ideas:
- Juan Pablo's story (Bytewing)
- Piedras Negras as a market that's just getting started
- The Colonial Iron Doors CRM story (real system that became Yeblanca OS)
- Why we started yeblanca

## Post Types & Templates

### The "Not For Everyone" Gatekeep

Used when you want to be clear about who we serve. This is our signature move.

**Structure:**
1. Statement of exclusion
2. Who we're NOT for (2-3 examples)
3. Who we ARE for (1-2 examples)
4. CTA

> *Template:*
>
> yeblanca is not for everyone.
>
> If you need [thing we don't do], we're not it.
>
> But if you [thing we do], that's exactly what we do.

---

### The "Comparison" Post

We win on clarity, not on attack.

**Structure:**
1. Frame: "Here's how we compare to the alternatives"
2. 3 columns: yeblanca / typical agency / DIY platforms
3. Keep it factual, keep it short

**Example from the site:**

| | yeblanca | Typical Agency | DIY Platforms |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who builds it | The engineer who scoped it | Junior dev on week 3 | You |
| Communication | Direct message | Account manager + status meetings | No one |
| Ownership | You choose (Hosted/Managed/Architect) | They own it | You own it, good luck |

---

### The "Specific Problem" Post

Pick one painful thing and show how we fix it.

**Structure:**
1. Describe the pain (concretely)
2. Why it happens
3. How we fix it (short)
4. CTA

> *Example:*
>
> You're losing clients because you forget to follow up.
>
> Not because you're lazy. Because you're busy running the business and there's no system reminding you.
>
> That's literally what we build. A system that tracks who hasn't come back and reminds you before they forget about you.
>
> Start here → [link]

---

### The "One Bold Claim" Post

A single sentence. Bold. No explanation needed. Let it sit.

> The engineer who scoped your project should be the one building it.
>
> — yeblanca

> "Bilingual" is not a differentiator. It's the minimum.
>
> — yeblanca

> You shouldn't need a developer to update your own website.
>
> — yeblanca

> 0% transaction fees. Open source. Yours forever.
>
> — yeblanca

---

### The "Behind the Build" Post

Show the work. Not the code — the thinking.

> We just shipped a project for [type of business]. The thing they said that stuck with me:
>
> "[quote from client]"
>
> That's why we do this. Not for the tech. For the moment when a system stops being a problem and starts being an advantage.

---

### The Bilingual / Code-Switch Post

Opens in one language, closes in the other. Reflects the border reality.

> *Example:*
>
> En Piedras Negras hay negocios que facturan millones de dólares al año y siguen llevando su operación en libretas y WhatsApp.
>
> No es falta de capacidad. Es falta de opciones que hablen su idioma — literal y figurativamente.
>
> We build systems for businesses that live on both sides of the border. Bilingual by default. Built to scale.
>
> Hablemos → [link]

## How to Adapt for Each Platform

### LinkedIn (Primary channel)

- Professional but not corporate
- Long-form posts work (100-300 words)
- Best pillars: Anti-Agency, Build It Right, Border Position
- Use the "One Bold Claim" as the opener, then expand
- Tag Juan Pablo? Only for founder content
- Hashtags: 3-5 max, relevant. `#WebDevelopment #TexasMexico #PiedrasNegras #CrossBorder`

**LinkedIn post structure that works:**

```
[One bold sentence — hook]

[2-3 sentences expanding the idea — keep each on its own line]

[Specific example or observation]

[The yeblanca take — what we do differently]

[CTA — subtle, link or "DM me"]
```

### Twitter/X

- Shorter. One idea per post.
- Best pillars: Bold Claim, Comparison, Behind the Build
- Threads work for "Comparison" posts
- Code-switch posts feel natural here
- Can be more provocative
- Hashtags: 1-2. Or none.

### Instagram

- Visual first. Text supports the image.
- Best pillars: Real Problems (can show UI/work), Founder/Origin, Behind the Build
- Captions: short. 1-3 lines. The image does the work.
- Use the typography (Space Grotesk + Space Mono + pink) on story templates
- Stories: Q&A, polls ("Hosted, Managed, or Architect?")

### Blog / Newsletter

- Longest form. 500-1000 words.
- Best pillars: Build It Right (deep dives), Border Position (long reads), Anti-Agency (manifesto-style)
- Tone can be more detailed but still direct. No fluff.
- Each post should have ONE takeaway. One.
